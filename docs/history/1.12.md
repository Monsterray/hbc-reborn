# HBC 1.12.x: findings and hardware results

What each 1.12.x change found, fixed and measured, newest first. The short list of
changes is in [CHANGELOG.md](../../CHANGELOG.md); open items are in
[review.md](../review.md).

## 1.12.2: the Mii drawn as a sliver, and two libogc 3.1.0 bugs under it

After a change of Mii, or the playground's Slap_03, the avatar's body drew
as a thin upright sliver, on the Wii and in Dolphin, in some builds and not
others. Poisoned heap and stack, ASan and UBSan on the PC, and a check of
every paired-single result against C found nothing; a Dolphin GDB
watchpoint on the bone matrices found the cause.

- **libogc's `ps_guMtxConcat` reads a word at `r13`.** `gu_psasm.S` loads
  `Unit01` (0, 1) with `psq_l UNIT01,Unit01@sdarel(r13),0,0`. `psq_l` has a
  12-bit offset, then the W bit and the I field; the 16-bit SDA relocation
  writes all of them. devkitPPC's linker scripts leave `_SDA_BASE_` at ld's
  default, `.sdata` + 32 KiB, so `Unit01`'s offset is -32768 and the
  instruction came out as `psq_l f31,0(r13),1,0`: one float from `r13`,
  1.0 for the other. Every concatenation added that float, times the
  translation, into its products. In HBC the word at `r13` was a particle
  of the Mii's effects, so the poof of a new Mii and Slap_03's dust bent
  the body. **Fixes:** `newrvl.ld` sets `_SDA_BASE_` at `.sdata`'s start
  (`tests/test_memory_layout.py` checks the base, that `Unit01` is within
  2047 bytes of it, and the instruction's offset, W and I); sdk/mii 1.4.1
  calls `c_guMtxConcat`, so apps on the stock scripts (mii_app, the
  example, outside users) are safe too, and its README says how to check
  an app. Reported as devkitPro/libogc#281 (load `Unit01` through a
  register). **Trigger:** when a release with the fix is taken, sdk/mii
  may go back to the paired-single product.
- **A thread that ends keeps the FPU.** libogc 3.1.0 switches the FPU
  lazily: SPRG3 names its owner, and `KThreadExit` never clears it. After
  `LWP_JoinThread` frees the thread, the next FP instruction anywhere saves
  the registers into the freed block. `fpurelease.h` clears the owner as
  the avatar loader's and the picker worker's last act (the threads that
  use floats); it refuses to build on another libogc. It was not the
  sliver (that build still sliced). Reported as devkitPro/libogc#282.
  **Trigger:** with any libogc past 3.1, check `KThreadExit` and drop the
  header if it clears SPRG3.
- **Kept from the hunt:** `make DEBUG_POISON=1` (`poison.c`: a NaN pattern
  in fresh and freed blocks and the unused stack); `make memmap`
  (`tools/memmap.py`: each symbol's address, size and object file).
- **The audit after** (HBC 1.12.3): the agent's network-option buffers
  and two Message Board read buffers are whole cache lines (libogc
  invalidates ioctlv vectors and read buffers after the call); a new Mii
  keeps the loaded effects and clears only what is playing, with the
  effect handles in one place; devnet's thread reads the avatar's body
  under a lock, as a reload frees it; the playground's captions are
  translated.

## 1.12.1: the levels where they look right, and the hair whole

The user watched Miis walk through the levels on the bench Wii and chose
where each should begin; and saw level 2 tear now and then on short hair.

- **`mii_app parade [SECONDS [COLS [GAP]]]`** (and `tests/wii_mii.py
  parade`): ranks of 20 Miis 900 apart walking round from the horizon to
  the bottom of the screen (out of sight before they come round again), then
  the other way, seen from higher up so the grass shows between the ranks.
  A line across the grass marks where each level begins for a Mii of middle
  height: white level 1, yellow 2, orange 3, red 4, found from
  `mii_body_lod_for` itself.
- **Where the levels begin** (`mii_body_lod_for`, sdk/mii 1.2.1): under 90,
  39, 30.5 and 25 pixels tall, was 150, 90, 50 and 25. Level 1 at the old
  level 2's point; level 2 halfway (in distance) from there to level 4's,
  level 3 halfway on. The tolerances stay half a pixel at the old points,
  so each level is drawn smaller than it was made for.
- **The hair cracked.** Its thin pieces (bangs, sideburns) folded apart
  and the sky showed through, under a pixel wide at level 2's size, so it
  sparkled as a Mii walked; worst on short styles.
  - A PC check (every one of the 72 styles, opaque parts on magenta from
    above-front, above-back and the side, a crack being a gap with the head
    on both sides): 47 crack pixels at level 2, 478 at level 3.
  - Not the forehead shrunk under coarse hair (off: 46 and 441), not the
    face's layers over the hair (21 pixels), not the hair's outline (kept
    whole: 47 and 464).
  - The hair now folds at half the tolerance: 6 and 243. About 30 more
    hair triangles a Mii at level 2.
  - Heads, 50 random Miis: 494 triangles to 376, 338, 267, 175 (were 357,
    306, 216, 124). Against the whole head at 90, 39, 30 and 25 pixels,
    colour off by 1.7, 2.9, 6.0, 13.9 of 255 and silhouettes 99.8, 99.5,
    98.6, 95.5% the same (level 4 was 24 and 91.4%).
- **Bench Wii**, parade (120 Miis, 120 s each way): 59.9 frames a second
  both ways, CPU 2.6 ms, GPU 4.1 ms a frame (3.9 before the hair change).

## 1.12.0: release; Miis by the hundred, and the SDK's audit

1.12.0 releases what 1.11.2 to 1.11.10 built in sdk/mii (`MIIDRAW_VERSION`
1.2.0): the Mii Channel's body skinned on the GPU with cross-fades, a Mii
that keeps only what it draws, parts shared between Miis, levels of detail
made from the user's own files, faces drawn by the GPU, and the drawing done
by the hardware. 200 walking Miis hold 60 fps on the bench Wii.

- **Audit of sdk/mii** (two independent reads of miidraw.c and miibody.c,
  the docs and their users). Fixed:
  - A body's display list was walked by its own counts: a damaged
    primitive count read past its object (into the next file bytes, and the
    levels of detail past the copied list). Each load and primitive must now
    fit in its object.
  - A second model in a `.brres` was read over the first (its arrays
    leaked, counts grew). The first only.
  - One bad animation made every animation after it fail (a sticky flag).
  - `mii_model_set_body` freed the built body before its own allocations:
    out of memory, a Mii had no body at all. It allocates first now, and on
    failure the model is unchanged.
  - `mii_body_make_lods` leaked its runs on a list with too many.
  - A head drawn at a level past those it was built with (body 4 levels,
    head 2, `pose.lod` 4) shrank its forehead and moved its face's layers by
    level 4's amount over level 2's triangles. The level is now clamped once,
    and one function (`head_mtx`) loads the head's matrices for both the
    display-list and the part-by-part paths.
  - Out of memory between a head level's two lists left the first: the
    nose line and glasses never drew again. Both or neither.
  - The GPU's texture cache was never invalidated on `mii_draw`'s path: a
    face built where a freed one was could show the old texels. The next
    draw after a texture is freed invalidates it.
  - `mii_strips` stops a strip at 65535 vertices (a GX count).
  - The whole body's triangle count now leaves out strips' turning
    triangles by position (each corner is its own skin vertex, so the
    index test never fired): `full_tris` is the real count.
  - Out of range, `pose.anim` stands at Wait's first frame, as documented.
- **Easier to use:**
  - `mii_pixels_tall(model, view, place, proj, viewport_h)`: how tall a Mii
    stands on screen, what `mii_body_lod_for` and the face size go by. The
    walk test used 120 units for a Mii's height; a Mii is about 170 (what
    the levels' half-pixel tolerances assume), so it chose levels a third
    too coarse. With the right sizes, bench Wii, 10 s each way: 200 Miis
    59.9 fps (CPU 3.8 ms, GPU 5.7 ms a frame), 400 Miis 38.3 fps (CPU 8.6
    ms, GPU 8.4 ms). 1.11.10's 48 fps for 400 was with the coarser levels.
  - miidraw.h's overview names the crowd path and current numbers (it said
    1 MiB a Mii and CPU faces); each struct says what is the app's and what
    the library's; `mii_draw` lists the matrices and arrays it changes
    (`GX_PNMTX1`, `GX_PNMTX2`, the matrix arrays); `mii_body_make_lods`
    says it frees the levels before; the pose functions say they write the
    body's memo; `mii_icon` clears to black.
  - sdk/mii/README.md: a crowd example (cache, levels, GPU faces, reuse).
  - docs/mii/README.md's CPU cost and docs/plans/mii-sdk-2.md's step 1 brought up to date.
- **Checked:** PC suite (198 tests); 16,000 damaged bodies under ASan and
  UBSan; bench Wii `mii_app draw` (paired-single pose within 0.00002 of
  the portable code, GPU faces within 0.08 of the CPU's on average, every
  level drawn, no failures) and `walk 200,400`.
